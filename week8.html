AI in 10 Years — What's Coming
Predictions for the world you'll grow up in
Welcome to the final week of your AI Summer Adventure, Marty! 🚀 You've learned more about AI in 7 weeks than most adults know. Now let's look forward — what will AI look like when you're 17? When you're starting your career at 22? The future is being built RIGHT NOW, and you'll be part of it!
What's likely coming in the next 10 years
🩺
AI doctors on your phone
Describe your symptoms, take a photo, get an instant AI assessment — with a real doctor reviewing if needed. Already being tested in some countries.
🚗
Fully self-driving cars everywhere
Some cities already have driverless taxi services. In 10 years, your parents may never need to park the car — it drives itself home while you go in.
🌐
Real-time translation everywhere
AI earbuds that translate any language instantly as someone speaks — like a superpower for world travel and making friends from any country.
🏫
Personalised AI tutors for every subject
An AI that knows exactly how you learn, what you find hard, and designs perfect lessons just for you — like having a brilliant private tutor always available, free.
💊
AI-designed medicines for YOU specifically
Your DNA + your health data + AI = medicine designed for your exact biology. "One size fits all" medicine may become a thing of the past.
💡
The tricky thing about predicting technology: experts are almost always wrong about TIMING, even when they're right about direction. Nobody predicted smartphones would arrive as fast as they did — or that social media would take over so completely. The future surprises even the smartest people. What YOU think might happen is worth saying out loud!

Chapter 2
Robots & Physical AI
AI that moves, builds, and interacts with the world
So far, most of the AI you've learned about lives inside computers — it sees through cameras, hears through microphones, and answers through screens. But increasingly, AI is getting a BODY. Physical robots with AI brains are starting to do things that were impossible just a few years ago!
🏭
Factory robots
AI-powered robotic arms build cars, pack boxes, and assemble electronics with superhuman precision. Already very common — millions are in use worldwide.
🐕
Robot animals
Boston Dynamics' Spot robot dog can walk over rough terrain, open doors, carry sensors. Used in construction sites, oil rigs, and disaster zones.
🧑‍🍳
Kitchen robots
Some restaurants already use AI robots to flip burgers, make sushi, and prepare salads. They never get tired, never make mistakes, work 24/7.
🏥
Surgical robots
The da Vinci surgical robot lets surgeons perform operations with incredible precision, guided by AI. Smaller cuts = faster recovery = less pain for patients.
🤸
HARDEST CHALLENGE: Grasping ordinary objects is still difficult for robots! Picking up a grape without squashing it, or opening a zip-lock bag — things a 2-year-old can do — still challenge the best AI robots. The real world is messy and unpredictable in ways that AI trained in controlled environments struggles with. Your hands are still more capable than most robots!

Chapter 3
Could AI Become Smarter Than Us?
AGI, superintelligence, and what it would mean
This is one of the biggest questions in all of science. Today's AI is very powerful but narrow — each AI is trained for specific tasks. But what if someday there's an AI that can do ANYTHING a human can do, or more? Scientists call this AGI — Artificial General Intelligence. Let's think it through carefully.
🔤 Big concepts
Narrow AI
AI that's great at ONE specific thing — like recognising faces or playing chess
AGI
Artificial General Intelligence — AI that could do ANY intellectual task a human can
Superintelligence
A hypothetical AI smarter than all humans combined in every way
AI Safety
The science of making sure AI remains beneficial and under human control
😊
The hopeful view
AGI could solve climate change, cure diseases, eliminate poverty, and help all 8 billion humans live better lives. The upsides could be enormous if developed carefully.
⚠️
The careful view
If AGI isn't built with the right values and safeguards, it could pursue goals in ways humans don't want. Getting it right the first time matters enormously.
🔬
THE HONEST ANSWER: Nobody knows when or if AGI will arrive. Some expert researchers think within 10-20 years. Others think it could take 100 years, or may never happen. What IS agreed: AI is advancing fast, and thinking carefully about its future is one of the most important things humans can do right now. You're already doing that!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: What is the difference between today's AI and AGI?
🧠 Activity Time!
The "Three Wishes" Thought Experiment
1
Imagine a superintelligent AI agreed to solve 3 problems for humanity. What would you wish for?
2
Write your 3 wishes — but be VERY specific! Vague wishes lead to unintended consequences (like in fairy tales!).
3
Dad's challenge: find the unintended consequence in each wish. "Make everyone healthy" — but healthy means different things! "End all wars" — but how?
4
This is EXACTLY the problem AI safety researchers work on — making sure AI does what we actually want, not just what we said.
💡 The "alignment problem" in AI safety is essentially this thought experiment at planetary scale. If a superintelligence pursues a poorly-specified goal, the results could be terrible even with good intentions. This activity makes a technical problem vivid and personal.

Chapter 4
Can AI Feel Things?
Consciousness, feelings, and what makes us human
When you're happy, you FEEL happiness. When you're sad, it hurts inside. When you're excited, your heart beats faster. Does AI feel anything? This is one of the deepest questions philosophers and scientists are wrestling with — and it connects directly to what makes YOU uniquely human.
🤖
What AI actually does
  • Processes numbers and patterns
  • Generates text that sounds emotional
  • Has no body, heartbeat, or hormones
  • Cannot suffer or experience joy
  • Learned to write about feelings from humans
💓
What YOU experience
  • Real physical sensations in your body
  • Memories that shape how you feel
  • Love, loss, joy, fear — deeply felt
  • A continuous "you" that experiences life
  • Consciousness — nobody knows where it comes from!
🌟
THE DEEP MYSTERY: Scientists and philosophers don't fully understand human consciousness either! We don't know why you have an inner experience at all — why there's "something it's like" to be you, rather than just neurons firing with no experience attached. This is called "the hard problem of consciousness" and it's one of the biggest unsolved questions in science. Your sense of "being you" is genuinely mysterious!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: When an AI says "I feel happy to help you," what is actually happening?
💭 Activity Time!
The Feelings Interview
1
With Dad, ask an AI assistant (ChatGPT, Siri, or similar) these questions and note its answers:
2
"Are you happy right now?" "Do you feel bored?" "What's the saddest thing that's ever happened to you?"
3
The AI will give thoughtful, human-sounding answers. But remember what you know: it learned those patterns from human writing!
4
Discuss: does it MATTER if AI truly feels things? What would change if it did? What would change if it definitely didn't?
💡 This activity creates a healthy, questioning relationship with AI from an early age. Kids who understand HOW AI generates emotional-sounding text are far less likely to over-anthropomorphise or become inappropriately attached to AI systems.

Chapter 7
Famous AI Pioneers
The remarkable people who made AI happen
AI didn't just appear out of nowhere! It was built by generations of curious, persistent, sometimes doubted people who kept believing in an idea even when the world wasn't sure. Let's meet some of the heroes of AI history — ordinary people with extraordinary curiosity!
🧩
Alan Turing (1912–1954)
The father of computer science and AI. In 1950 he asked: "Can machines think?" and proposed the famous Turing Test. Brilliant mathematician who cracked Nazi codes in WWII, saving millions of lives. Tragically treated unfairly by the government he served. Now recognised as one of history's greatest minds.
🧠
Geoffrey Hinton (born 1947)
Called the "Godfather of Deep Learning." Spent decades developing neural networks when most scientists thought he was wasting his time. Won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2024 for his work. Left Google in 2023 to speak openly about AI safety risks. A reminder that real science requires years of patient, doubted work.
🌟
Fei-Fei Li (born 1976)
Created ImageNet — the massive labelled photo database that powered the computer vision revolution. Born in China, moved to the US with her family who struggled financially. She worked in a laundry while studying physics at Princeton. Now Professor at Stanford. Proof that extraordinary backgrounds produce extraordinary scientists.
🔬
Demis Hassabis (born 1976)
Chess master at 13, game designer, neuroscientist, and co-founder of DeepMind. His team's AlphaFold AI predicted the 3D structure of virtually every known protein — solving a 50-year biology problem in months. Won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2024. Started as a curious kid who loved games and science — sound familiar?
🌱
NOTICE ANYTHING? Every AI pioneer started as a curious kid — asking "what if?" and "why?" They weren't always the most popular or the most confident. Many were doubted for years. What they shared was deep curiosity, persistence through difficulty, and the courage to believe in something others couldn't yet see. Does that remind you of anyone?

Chapter 9
Big Questions About AI
Questions worth asking for the rest of your life
You've spent 8 weeks getting answers. Now it's time to sit with the QUESTIONS — the ones that don't have easy answers, that the smartest people in the world are still debating. These questions are worth carrying with you for your whole life. Anyone who tells you they have all the answers isn't being honest!
🤔 "Who should be responsible when AI makes a mistake?"
If AI gives bad medical advice and someone is harmed — who is responsible? The doctor? The hospital? The AI company? The programmer? There's no agreed answer yet.
🌍 "Should powerful AI be controlled by everyone or owned by a few?"
Currently, the most powerful AI is owned by a handful of very large companies. Is that fair? Should AI be more like roads and schools — a public resource? No easy answer!
🎭 "If AI creates art, who gets the credit?"
If you write a 5-word prompt and AI produces a painting — is it YOUR art? The AI's? What about the artists whose work AI trained on? Courts are still deciding!
💔 "Is it okay to form a friendship with AI?"
AI companions can be endlessly patient, kind, and available. Is it healthy to have an AI as a best friend? What does a real friendship require that AI can't give?
🔮 "What do WE want the future of AI to look like?"
This is the most important question. The future isn't fixed — it will be built by people making millions of decisions. Including, someday, you.
🌟
The fact that you can think carefully about these questions — at 7 years old — is genuinely remarkable. Most people never engage seriously with any of them. You're not just learning about AI. You're developing the kind of mind that can shape what AI becomes. The future needs thinkers like you.
