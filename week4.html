Chapter 1
Meet the AI Team
The people who build AI together
Building AI isn't a one-person job! It takes an entire team of brilliant people with different skills — kind of like how building a house needs architects, builders, electricians, and designers. Let's meet all the people who make AI happen!
🔬
AI Researcher
Invents new AI techniques. Asks "what if we tried this?" and runs experiments. Usually has a PhD.
💻
ML Engineer
Builds and trains the actual AI models. Writes the code that makes AI learn from data.
📊
Data Scientist
Collects, cleans, and prepares all the training data. Remembers: garbage in, garbage out!
⚖️
AI Ethicist
Makes sure AI is fair, safe, and doesn't harm people. Asks "should we build this?" not just "can we?"
🎨
UX Designer
Designs how humans interact with AI — makes it easy and enjoyable to use.
🧪
QA Tester
Tries to break the AI! Finds bugs, errors, and unfair results before millions of people use it.
🌟
A BIG AI team at a company like Google or Anthropic might have hundreds of people — engineers, scientists, designers, lawyers, and ethicists. Building AI responsibly is a massive team effort!

Chapter 2
What is Code?
The language computers understand
Code is a set of instructions written in a special language that computers can follow. Just like how you could write a recipe in English for a human chef, programmers write recipes in Python or JavaScript for computer "chefs." Let's see what code actually looks like!
Real Python code — a loop!
# This tells the computer to count from 1 to 5
for number in range(1, 6):
    print("Hello Marty, number:", number)
🔤 Coding words
Python
The most popular programming language for AI — named after Monty Python, not a snake!
Variable
A named box that stores a value — like "score = 10"
Loop
Instructions that repeat — "do this 1000 times"
Function
A named set of instructions you can use over and over
🐍
Python is used by almost every major AI team in the world! Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta — they all use Python as their main language. And the amazing thing? You can start learning Python for FREE at scratch.mit.edu or code.org — both designed for kids your age!

Q1: What is Python (in programming)?

Write Your Name in Binary!
1
Computers only understand 1s and 0s — called binary. Every letter has a binary code!
2
M = 01001101, A = 01000001, R = 01010010, T = 01010100, Y = 01011001
3
Write MARTY in binary on paper: 01001101 01000001 01010010 01010100 01011001
4
That's 40 ones and zeros just for your name! Computers do this for EVERYTHING.

Chapter 3
If/Then Rules
The basic building block of all programs
Every computer program — from the simplest app to the most complex AI — is built on one basic idea: IF something is true, THEN do something. It's called a conditional statement, and once you understand it, you can start thinking like a programmer!
If/Then examples everywhere
# A simple thermostat program
if temperature < 68:
    turn_on_heating()
elif temperature > 75:
    turn_on_cooling()
else:
    do_nothing()
🌧️
Weather app
IF it will rain THEN show an umbrella icon. IF temperature below 32° THEN show snow warning.
🎮
Video game
IF player health = 0 THEN game over. IF player collects coin THEN add 10 points.
📱
Face ID
IF face matches stored map THEN unlock device. IF face doesn't match THEN stay locked.
🤖
Simple chatbot
IF user says "hello" THEN reply "Hi there!" IF user asks weather THEN check weather app.
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q1: What is an "if/then" statement in programming?
🎮 Activity Time!
Program Your Morning Routine!
1
Write your morning routine as IF/THEN rules on paper.
2
Example: IF I wake up before 7am THEN I have time for cereal. IF it's raining THEN I take an umbrella.
3
Write at least 5 IF/THEN rules for your morning.
4
Congrats — you just wrote your first program! (In English, but the logic is the same as real code.)
💡 This activity is literally how beginner programmers think. The skill of breaking decisions into conditions is called "computational thinking" and it's foundational for coding!

Decision Trees
How computers make choices step by step
Imagine a giant flowchart with YES/NO questions at each branch. That's a Decision Tree! It's one of the most popular types of AI because it's easy to understand — you can actually SEE the logic. Let's build one to identify animals!
Animal decision tree 🐾
Does it have wings?
YES ↓
Can it fly?
YES
🐦 Bird
NO
🐧 Penguin
NO ↓
Does it bark?
YES
🐕 Dog
NO
🐱 Cat
🏥
REAL WORLD: Early medical AI used decision trees! "Does the patient have a fever? YES → Does the patient have a rash? YES → Could be X, Y, or Z diseases." Simple but effective. Today's AI uses much more complex versions of this idea!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q1: What is a decision tree in AI?
🌳 Activity Time!

💡 Good starting questions: "Does it have 4 legs?" "Does it live in water?" "Is it bigger than a dog?" This is literally how classification algorithms work in ML!

Chapter 5
Neural Networks
Inspired by YOUR brain's neurons
The most powerful AI today is built on something called neural networks — and the inspiration came directly from studying how YOUR brain works! Your brain has 86 billion neurons all connected together. AI engineers built a simplified version of this in code. Let's explore!
🔤 Key words
Neuron (brain)
A brain cell that receives signals, processes them, and fires (or doesn't)
Artificial Neuron
A maths function in code that mimics what a brain neuron does
Weight
A number that controls how much influence one neuron has on the next
Network
All the artificial neurons connected together in layers
🧠
Real brain neuron
Receives electrical signals from thousands of other neurons. If the combined signal is strong enough, it fires a new signal to the next neurons.
💻
Artificial neuron
Receives numbers from the previous layer. Multiplies them by "weights", adds them up. If the total is high enough, it passes a signal to the next layer.
🔢
A large AI like ChatGPT has about 175 BILLION artificial neurons — that's roughly TWICE the number of real neurons in your brain! But your brain is still far more efficient — it runs on 20 watts while ChatGPT needs thousands of watts of electricity.
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q1: What inspired the design of neural networks?
🕸️ Activity Time!
Build a Human Neural Network!
1
Get a ball of yarn and gather 3+ family members.
2
Each person is a "neuron." Hold one end of the yarn and pass the ball to someone else.
3
Keep passing until everyone is connected — you've made a neural network!
4
Now pass a "signal" (a squeeze on the yarn) from one person to another. That's how information flows through a neural network!
💡 You can make this more realistic: tell some people to only pass the squeeze if they receive it from TWO people at the same time — that's the "threshold" concept in neural networks!

Chapter 6
Layers in a Brain Network
Input, hidden layers, output — like a relay race!
A neural network isn't just a random tangle of neurons — it's organised into LAYERS! Think of it like a relay race: the first runner (input layer) starts with the raw data, passes it through several middle runners (hidden layers), and the last runner (output layer) crosses the finish line with the final answer!
A neural network recognising a handwritten number
INPUT
LAYER
784 neurons
(28×28 pixels)
→
HIDDEN
LAYER 1
512 neurons
(finds edges)
→
HIDDEN
LAYER 2
256 neurons
(finds shapes)
→
OUTPUT
LAYER
10 neurons
(one per digit 0-9)
🏃
DEEP LEARNING: When a neural network has many hidden layers (sometimes hundreds!), it's called "deep learning" — that's where the "deep" in "deep neural network" comes from. Each extra layer lets AI understand more complex and abstract things. More layers = deeper understanding!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q1: What is "deep learning"?
🏃 Activity Time!
Relay Race = Data Through Layers!
1
Set up a relay race with 4 stations in your garden or hallway.
2
Station 1 (input): shout a number. Station 2: double it. Station 3: add 10. Station 4 (output): say the final answer!
3
Each station is a "layer" — it takes the data, transforms it, and passes it on.
4
Try with different starting numbers. That's data flowing through a neural network!
💡 For extra realism, have each person "forget" the original number — they can only work with what they received. This is exactly how neural networks work — each layer only sees the output from the previous layer!

Chapter 7
How Much Power Does AI Need?
Huge computers and lots of electricity
Training a big AI model requires ENORMOUS amounts of electricity — more than you'd believe! While your brain runs on just 20 watts (less than a light bulb), training a large AI model uses as much electricity as thousands of homes in a whole year. This is one of the big challenges facing AI today!
🧠
Your brain
20 watts
Less than a dim light bulb. Runs on glucose from the food you eat. The most energy-efficient computer ever!
💻
Your iPad
~20 watts
Roughly the same as your brain — that's why Apple works so hard on energy efficiency in their chips!
🏢
AI training server
~300 watts
One high-end AI chip uses 300 watts. A training cluster might have thousands of these!
🌍
Training GPT-3
1,287 MWh
Training GPT-3 used about as much energy as 120 US homes use in a whole year. That's just ONE training run!
🌱
THE GOOD NEWS: AI researchers are working hard to make AI more efficient. Some companies now train AI using renewable energy (solar and wind). And new chip designs are making AI far more energy-efficient every year. Energy-efficient AI is one of the most important challenges for your generation to solve!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q1: How does your brain's energy use compare to an AI training server?
⚡ Activity Time!
Light Bulb vs. Brain vs. AI!
1

Turn it on and look at how dim it is. Your incredible brain — with all its creativity and feelings — runs on THAT.

Chapter 8
AI in the Cloud
Why AI lives on the internet
When you ask Siri a question, your iPad doesn't do all the thinking alone — it sends your question to a giant building full of computers somewhere in the world. This is called "the cloud." But what exactly is the cloud, and why does AI live there instead of on your device?
📱
On-device AI
Simple AI that runs directly on your iPad. Fast, works offline, private. But limited to what fits in a small chip.
Examples: Face ID, autocorrect, Siri basic commands
☁️
Cloud AI
Your question travels to giant server buildings (data centres). Thousands of powerful computers work on it. The answer comes back in milliseconds!
Examples: ChatGPT, Google Search, Siri complex questions
🌍
DATA CENTRES: The buildings that run the cloud are enormous! A single data centre might be as big as 10 football fields, use as much electricity as a small city, and need millions of litres of water to keep the computers cool. There are thousands of these buildings around the world running 24/7!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q1: When you use ChatGPT, where does the AI thinking happen?
☁️ Activity Time!
Draw the Cloud Journey!
1
Draw your home on the left side of a big paper.
2
Draw a huge building full of computers somewhere in the middle (the data centre).
3
Draw the path your question travels: iPad → WiFi router → internet cables → data centre → back to you!
4

💡 The data centre might actually be in a different country! Google and Amazon have data centres on every continent. The speed of light through fibre optic cables is what makes this seem instantaneous.

Chapter 9
The Future of AI Hardware
Chips, quantum computers, and what comes next
The chips inside computers have been getting smaller and faster for 60 years — but we're approaching the limits of how small traditional chips can get. Scientists are now exploring wild new ideas for the next generation of AI hardware. Welcome to the future!
🔲
GPU (Graphics chip)
Originally made for video games! Turned out to be perfect for AI because they can do millions of maths operations in parallel. NVIDIA GPUs power most AI today.
🧠
Neuromorphic chips
Chips designed to mimic the brain — they use tiny bursts of electricity just like real neurons. Ultra energy-efficient! Intel's Loihi chip is an example.
⚛️
Quantum computers
Use quantum physics to do certain calculations exponentially faster. Not yet powerful enough for AI, but potentially revolutionary in 10-20 years!
💡
Photonic AI
Uses light instead of electricity! Incredibly fast and energy-efficient. Still experimental but very promising for future AI.
⚛️
QUANTUM COMPUTERS: Instead of bits (0 or 1), quantum computers use "qubits" that can be 0, 1, or BOTH at the same time (thanks to quantum physics!). This lets them explore millions of possibilities simultaneously. When mature, they could solve problems that would take regular computers millions of years. You might work on this technology someday, Marty!
