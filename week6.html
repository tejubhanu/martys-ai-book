Chapter 1
What is Fairness?
How we decide what's right and wrong
Before we can talk about whether AI is fair, we need to think about what 'fairness' actually means! Is it treating everyone exactly the same? Or treating everyone according to their needs? These are questions philosophers have debated for thousands of years — and now AI has to answer them too.
💡
Here's a classic fairness puzzle: Three kids are watching a baseball game over a fence. One is tall and can see fine. One is medium height and can just about see. One is short and can't see at all. If you give them all the same size box to stand on, is that FAIR? Or should the short kid get a bigger box?

💡 The equality vs equity distinction is fundamental to AI ethics — it's why AI can't just be 'neutral' or 'treat everyone the same.' This is a deeply important concept worth spending extra time on!

Chapter 2
When AI is Unfair
Real examples of AI making unfair choices
Because AI learns from human data, it can learn and repeat human biases — sometimes making things even worse. Let's look at some real examples of when AI got fairness badly wrong, and what we can learn from these mistakes.
Real AI bias examples
📸 Face recognition
Worked worse on darker skin tones because training data had fewer examples
💼 Job hiring AI
Recommended fewer women for technical jobs because past hiring data was biased
🏛️ Criminal justice AI
Predicted higher risk scores for certain groups unfairly
🏥 Medical AI
Recommended less pain treatment for certain patient groups
💡
None of these AI systems were designed to be unfair — the bias crept in through biased training data and biased human decisions baked into history. This is why AI ethics is so important. When we automate unfairness, it can happen at massive scale — affecting millions of people!

Chapter 3
Privacy & AI
What AI knows about us — and should it?
Every time you use an app, search online, watch a video, or talk to a voice assistant, you're creating DATA about yourself. Companies use AI to collect and analyse this data to understand you better than you might understand yourself! Is this OK? Let's think carefully about privacy.
Data you create every day
📱
Every app you open and how long you use it
🔍
Everything you search for online
📍
Your location every minute of the day
🎵
Every song you play and when you skip
💬
Messages and conversations
💡
By analysing your data, AI can often predict things about you that you've never even said out loud — your mood, your health, even your political views. One study showed an app could predict depression weeks before the person showed any symptoms, just from phone usage patterns. Powerful — and a little scary!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: What is one important thing you learned in this chapter?
🎯 Activity Time!
privacy reflection activity
1
Write a list of 10 things about yourself.
2
Now mark each one: would you share this with: your best friend? Your teacher? A stranger? An app?
3
Are there things you'd share with friends but NOT an app?
4
Discuss: why might the same information feel different depending on who's collecting it?
💡 This activity develops data literacy and privacy instincts. The key insight: context matters enormously — sharing location with a friend to meet up is very different from an app silently tracking your location all day.

Chapter 4
Who Owns Your Data?
When you use apps, your info travels far
Here's a question most adults never think about: when you sign up for a free app, who owns all the data the app collects about you? When something is free, YOU are usually the product — your data is what the company is getting paid with. Let's understand what happens to your data!
The journey of your data
📱 You use the app
📊 App collects your data
🏢 Sent to company servers
🤖 AI analyses it
💰 Sold to advertisers
📣 Ads target you
💡
When you use a 'free' social media app, the company makes money by showing you targeted ads. The AI learns what you like, predicts what you'll want to buy, and shows you ads for exactly those things. Your attention and your personal data are the price you pay for using the app 'for free.'
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: What is one important thing you learned in this chapter?
🎯 Activity Time!
data journey activity
1
Pick one free app you use. Draw it on the left of a paper.
2
Now draw all the companies that might receive your data from that app.
3
Draw lines showing the data journey: app → analytics company → advertiser → you (with ads).
4
Most free apps share data with 10-100+ other companies! Does knowing this change how you feel about using them?
💡 Many parents don't understand the data economy either — this activity teaches both parent and child something genuinely important. The '10-100 companies' statistic is real and consistently shocks people when they first encounter it.

Chapter 5
AI & Jobs
What happens when robots do some jobs?
One of the biggest questions about AI is: will it take people's jobs? Some jobs that humans do today will probably be automated by AI. But historically, new technology has always created new types of jobs too. Let's think honestly about what might change and what that means for your future!
💡
A famous study estimated that about 47% of current jobs could be automated in the next 20 years. But the same study showed that throughout history, technology has always created more jobs than it destroyed — just different types. The key is: which jobs are HARDEST for AI to do?
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: What is one important thing you learned in this chapter?
🎯 Activity Time!
future thinking activity
1
List 10 jobs you know (teacher, doctor, driver, chef, artist...)
2
For each, rate how hard it would be for AI: 🟢 easy, 🟡 medium, 🔴 hard
3
Generally: routine, predictable tasks = easier for AI. Creative, emotional, unpredictable = harder.
4
Surprise: doctors and lawyers have parts that AI can do! Plumbers and social workers are much harder for AI.
💡 The counter-intuitive finding that high-education jobs are often MORE automatable than skilled trades is genuinely surprising to most adults and creates great discussion. A robot can analyse an X-ray but struggles to fix a leaking pipe!

Chapter 6
New Jobs AI Creates
Future careers in the AI age
For every job AI makes easier or replaces, it tends to create new jobs that didn't exist before. Just like the internet created jobs like 'web designer' and 'social media manager' that nobody could have predicted in 1990, AI is creating entirely new careers right now!
New jobs that barely existed 5 years ago
🤖 Prompt Engineer
Crafts the perfect instructions to get the best results from AI
⚖️ AI Ethicist
Ensures AI systems are fair, safe, and legal
🔍 AI Auditor
Tests AI systems to find hidden biases and errors
🎓 AI Trainer
Teaches AI by labelling data and rating its outputs
🛡️ AI Safety Researcher
Works to make sure powerful AI remains safe and beneficial
💡
The most exciting thing: many of the most important AI jobs of 2035 probably haven't been invented yet — just like how 'app developer' didn't exist in 2005. The best preparation isn't learning specific AI tools (they'll change) but developing curiosity, critical thinking, and creativity — skills AI can't replace!

💡 Future job design is a wonderful creativity and futurism exercise. Kids often invent genuinely plausible jobs — and it opens their minds to the fact that their career might be something that doesn't exist yet!

Chapter 7
Fake Pictures & Deepfakes
AI that makes fake photos and videos
AI can now create photos and videos of people doing and saying things that NEVER HAPPENED — and they look completely real. These are called deepfakes. A deepfake video could show a famous person saying something terrible, even if they never said it. This is one of the most challenging problems in the AI age!
💡
In 2023, AI-generated fake images spread rapidly on social media appearing to show a famous politician being arrested — before any such event occurred. Millions of people saw them before anyone realised they were fake. Being able to identify and question potentially fake images is an essential skill for your generation!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: What is one important thing you learned in this chapter?
🎯 Activity Time!
media literacy activity
1
Dad: search online for 'AI generated face' or use thispersondoesnotexist.com
2
Look at the faces together. They look incredibly real — because they are AI-generated!
3
Now look closely for clues: weird backgrounds, strange ears, blurry teeth, odd hair.
4
Make a list: what signs might tell you an image is AI-generated vs. real?
💡 thispersondoesnotexist.com generates a new photorealistic AI face on every refresh. Every single face is completely fake — there is no such person. The realism is genuinely startling even for adults and creates an excellent jumping-off point for media literacy discussions.

Chapter 8
Telling Real from Fake
How to be a smart digital detective
In a world where AI can create fake photos, videos, and news articles, the most important skill you can develop is critical thinking. How do you tell what's real from what's fake? Let's learn some strategies that will serve you your whole life!
The SIFT method for checking information
🛑 Stop
Don't share immediately — take a breath and think
🔍 Investigate the source
Who made this? Are they trustworthy?
🔎 Find better coverage
Can you find the same story on trusted news sites?
🌳 Trace claims
Where did this originally come from? Follow it back
💡
One simple rule: If something seems designed to make you feel VERY angry or VERY scared, slow down! Fake news is often crafted to trigger strong emotions so you share it without thinking. Strong emotions make us less critical. Real news can be upsetting — but fake news is specifically designed to manipulate feelings.
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: What is one important thing you learned in this chapter?
🎯 Activity Time!
media literacy activity
1
Dad: write 5 news headlines — some real, some made up but plausible.
2
Marty: decide which are real and which are fake — and explain WHY you think so.
3
What clues helped you decide? Extreme language? Unbelievable claims?
4
Now look them up! Were you right? What fooled you?
💡 This game is endlessly playable and directly builds the most critical media literacy skill: instinctive questioning of claims. Doing this repeatedly with increasing subtlety is genuinely one of the most important things a parent can teach a child today.

Chapter 9
AI Rules — Who Makes Them?
Governments, companies, scientists, and YOU
Who decides what AI is allowed to do and what it's not? Right now, different countries, companies, and organisations all have different rules — and there's a big global conversation happening about how to make sure powerful AI is safe and beneficial for everyone. Including you!
Who shapes AI rules
🏛️ Governments
Make laws about what AI can and can't do in their countries
🏢 Companies
Set their own internal rules and safety policies
🔬 Researchers
Publish safety guidelines and identify risks before they happen
🌍 International orgs
Try to create global agreements about AI safety
👤 You!
Public opinion shapes what politicians and companies do
💡
The amazing thing: this is still being decided RIGHT NOW. The rules for AI are being written by people alive today — including some who started just as curious as you are! By the time you're an adult, you could literally be one of the people who helps write the rules for AI. Your voice matters!
🎯 Quick Quiz!
Q: What is one important thing you learned in this chapter?
🎯 Activity Time!
civic activity
1
If you were in charge of AI, what rules would you create?
2
Think about: what should AI never be allowed to do? What should it always have to do?
3
Write 3 rules that you think every AI must follow.
4
Share your rules with the family. Hold a vote — which rules would everyone agree to?
💡 The key message: this conversation is genuinely unfinished. Children who engage with these questions today are better equipped to participate in the democratic process around AI as they grow up. Empowerment over helplessness!
